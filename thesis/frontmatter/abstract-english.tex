\unnumberedchapter{ABSTRACT}

\begin{english}

Electronic commerce businesses record a complete transaction history, yet that
record is rarely used to support marketing and sales decisions. This thesis
designs and implements a system that extracts intelligent analytics from such
data using data mining and machine learning techniques.

The system answers seven questions raised by actual online retail clients and
applies three families of techniques: frequent itemset mining with the Apriori
and FP-Growth algorithms to surface product associations, two-level clustering
that first groups by region and time and then re-clusters the customers within
each group by RFM features, and time series forecasting with exponential
smoothing. The number of clusters is selected by silhouette score rather than
fixed in advance, and every forecast is evaluated out of sample against a naive
baseline.

The analysis extends to economic indicators, decomposing revenue change into
six flows and measuring how concentrated that revenue is across customers.

The architecture separates ingestion from analysis through a published data
contract. A store is attached by sending its transactions in that form or by
granting read access to its own database, so onboarding is configuration
rather than code, and no analytical code changes.

A central methodological choice is the separation of measured from assumed
quantities. Because the data contains neither cost of goods nor marketing
spend, profitability is not computed as a measurement but as a sensitivity
analysis over explicitly stated assumptions, isolated from the measured metrics
at every level of the system.

\vspace{1cm}

{\bfseries Keywords}: data mining, electronic commerce, frequent itemsets,
clustering, business intelligence

\end{english}
